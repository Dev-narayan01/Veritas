Library Automation — sample note 25

Library automation began as a way to replace paper catalogues with searchable records. Early systems stored only titles and call numbers, and staff still walked the shelves to check availability. Modern systems add borrowing history, reservation queues and shelf location maps, which makes the catalogue a working tool rather than a static list.

Planting programmes fail most often at the maintenance stage. A tree needs water for the first three summers and protection from construction work, and both are easy to forget once the photographs of planting day have been taken.
